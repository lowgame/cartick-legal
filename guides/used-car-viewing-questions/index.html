What to ask before a used-car viewing

A question sheet you can print or keep on your phone, from the first seller message to the independent inspection.

By Ahmet Kamer ÇİVİ, creator of CarTick · 12 September 2026

A viewing is easier to organize when you ask a few clear questions before travelling. This sheet helps you keep the seller’s answers together and decide what needs independent verification. It is a preparation tool, not a vehicle assessment.

Before you arrange the visit

  • Is the vehicle still available, and who is selling it?
  • Can you share the VIN and the details needed to verify the vehicle independently?
  • What service records are available?
  • What known faults, warning lights, repairs or accident history should I know about?
  • Can an independent mechanic inspect the vehicle before I decide?

Record the answer, the date and what still needs to be checked. A reassuring answer is not the same as a document or independent inspection. Vehicle-history and title requirements vary by location; use the relevant official services for your jurisdiction.

Keep claims and evidence separate

Seller answers and follow-up questions
Seller saysEvidence to reviewQuestion for mechanic
“Recently serviced”Invoice, date and mileageWhat work was done, and what is still due?

“Repair completed”Repair documentationCan you assess the repair and any remaining concerns?

At the viewing: record, then ask

Use CarTick’s Quick Scan to record visible concerns and keep your photos and notes attached to the right car. Mark an uncertainty as a question instead of converting it into a confident diagnosis. Ask permission for photos and avoid capturing the seller’s personal documents unnecessarily.

Keep any physical checks within your knowledge and safe conditions. Do not get under an unsupported vehicle, touch hot or moving components, or handle your phone while driving. Leave technical inspection to a qualified professional.

Arrange the independent inspection

Ask the mechanic what their pre-purchase inspection covers, how they will report findings, and how you can provide your notes beforehand. In its used-car guidance, the US Federal Trade Commission recommends an independent mechanical inspection even for a vehicle described as certified.

If the seller refuses independent verification, pause. Decide what information you still need before making a commitment. An app cannot settle title status, certify safety or guarantee a purchase outcome.
