What to compare before a second viewing

  1. Identify the exact car. Write down the year, trim and seller reference so notes from two similar listings do not get mixed up.
  2. Keep the units consistent. Include the currency with price and miles or kilometres with mileage. The form does not convert either.
  3. Record the source of each claim. “Full history” in an advert and a set of dated invoices are different evidence.
  4. Write down the unanswered question. A blank history entry is unknown, not proof that nothing happened.

Turn the comparison into questions

If Car A has a lower asking price but an unexplained repair, keep both facts visible. Write the question you need answered rather than assigning an optimistic repair cost. If Car B has invoices, record which dates and mileage readings you actually saw; do not turn “some records” into “complete history”.

Take these notes to an independent mechanic. The US Federal Trade Commission’s used-car buying guide recommends an independent inspection and explains why a history report does not replace one. Its dealer and legal guidance is US-specific; check the rules where you are buying.
